Confidence Intervals

1. What is a Confidence Interval?

A random interval [L, U] that contains the true parameter θ with probability 1−α.

P(L ≤ θ ≤ U) = 1 − α

Interpretation: If we repeat sampling many times, (1−α)×100% of intervals will contain θ.

Common trap: "There is 95% probability θ is in [2.1, 3.4]" is WRONG. θ is fixed. The interval is random.

2. Key Terms

  • Confidence level (1−α): typically 90%, 95%, 99%
  • Coverage probability: P(interval contains θ)
  • Margin of error: half-width of CI

3. CI for Population Mean μ

σ known — use Z

X̄ₙ ± z_{α/2} · σ/√n

σ unknown — use t-distribution

X̄ₙ ± t_{α/2, n−1} · S/√n

where S = sample standard deviation, degrees of freedom = n−1

4. CI for Population Proportion p

p̂ ± z_{α/2} · √(p̂(1−p̂)/n)

where p̂ = x/n (sample proportion). Valid when np̂ ≥ 5 and n(1−p̂) ≥ 5.

5. Sample Size Determination

To estimate μ within margin E with confidence 1−α (σ known):

n = (z_{α/2} · σ / E)²

For proportion with margin E:

n = z²_{α/2} · p(1−p) / E²    (use p=0.5 for worst case)

7. Exam Checklist

  • Construct CI for μ (decide Z vs t)
  • Construct CI for proportion p
  • Find required sample size n
  • Interpret CI correctly in words
  • Relate CI to hypothesis testing (if μ₀ outside CI, reject H₀ at level α)
